# IMAGO Reflection Questions Bank

Five per archetype, 50 total. Used in the Reflection & Integration section of the IMAGO report. Also reusable by counselors in session.

These questions are not a diagnostic checklist. They are an invitation — a slow walk through your own wiring, asking whether what you see is the Spirit's work or the self's strategy. Read them as you would read Psalm 139:23-24: "Search me, O God, and know my heart! Try me and know my thoughts! And see if there be any grievous way in me, and lead me in the way everlasting."

Come honestly. There is no correct answer to impress anyone. The One who formed this particular wiring in you already knows what He will find.

## 1. The Shepherd

*Wiring: High Grace-bearing + very high Ortho-emotion (steady). Core shadow: absorbing weight that belongs to God.*

**Question 1:** Think of the person in your life who weighs on you most heavily right now — the one whose suffering you carry through the day like a stone in your pocket. When you sit with their pain, are you mediating it toward God, or are you absorbing it yourself? There is a difference between standing between someone and their sorrow long enough to point them toward the One who bears all sorrows, and simply taking the sorrow into yourself as though your carrying it somehow helps. Read Psalm 68:19: "Blessed be the Lord, who daily bears us up; God is our salvation." Does your compassion flow from that anchor, or does it bypass it?

*Source: after Powlison, "Making All Things New" — heart-idol X-ray; the shepherd who absorbs rather than mediates replaces God rather than glorifying Him.*

**Question 2:** If you subtracted your steadiness — your ability to remain calm when others are distressed, to stay at the bedside when others leave — would you still know that you are loved by God? What does your answer tell you about whether your steadiness is a gift freely given or a role you perform to feel necessary? Bridges asks the hardest version of this question in "Who Am I?": your identity is not your function in other people's suffering. You are loved before you show up for anyone.

*Source: after Bridges, "Who Am I?" — identity-in-Christ questions; the gift can become the ground of worth.*

**Question 3:** When was the last time you let someone else shepherd you? Name the person, and then ask honestly: why was it hard? The high-Compassion, high-Courtesy wiring that makes you a remarkable presence in suffering can quietly produce a belief that you are the one who serves, never the one who receives. But the Good Shepherd of John 10 calls himself the door through which sheep go in and out — not just out. When did you last go in?

*Source: John 10:9; after Welch, "When People Are Big and God Is Small" — the Shepherd whose identity is bound up in being needed will resist being known as needy.*

**Question 4:** In your most recent season of sustained presence with someone in pain — a long illness, a grief, a counseling relationship — what did it cost you, and who knew? If no one knew, ask yourself why. Hiddenness in service can be holy, but it can also be a way of maintaining a private claim: "I am the one who never complains, who never burdens others, who simply endures." That is not grace — that is self-sufficiency dressed in pastoral clothes. Name one person who has permission to ask how you are actually doing, and commit to telling them the truth this week.

*Source: after Powlison, "Seeing with New Eyes" — the question of what interpretation sits beneath the behavior; also Hebrews 4:15-16, where even the great High Priest "sympathizes," but does not absorb.*

**Question 5:** Read Matthew 11:28-30, the words of the One whose compassion has no shadow side: "Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you, and learn from me, for I am gentle and lowly in heart." The call to the Shepherd is not only to extend this invitation to others but to receive it personally. When did you last come to Jesus as someone laboring and heavy laden, not as someone helping others find the way there? What would it mean to learn from him — not only his methods but his rest?

*Source: Matthew 11:28-30; after Tripp, "New Morning Mercies" — the pastoral voice that consistently reminds the caregiver that they are first a recipient of grace.*

## 2. The Mason

*Wiring: High Mastery (both aspects) + high Intellect + introverted. Core shadow: building to prove worth rather than from security in love.*

**Question 1:** Think about the last project you completed well — the one that came together and showed the marks of your care. When it was finished, what did you feel? Relief, satisfaction, pleasure, rest — these are gifts. But if what you felt most was a temporary reprieve from an underlying anxiety about whether you are enough, that is worth sitting with. Powlison calls this the X-ray question: what does the behavior reveal about what the heart believes it needs? Your building is good. The question is whether you believe you are loved before the building begins.

*Source: after Powlison, "Making All Things New," ch. 3 — X-ray questions that surface the heart's functional beliefs beneath productive behavior.*

**Question 2:** Read Nehemiah 4:6: "So we built the wall. And all the wall was joined together to half its height, for the people had a mind to work." Notice what Nehemiah does not say: he does not say the people worked because they were anxious about their legacy or their reputation among the nations. They had a mind to work — a willing, free motivation. Is your building like that? Or does the project feel, if you are honest, more like a way of proving something — to your father, to the church, to yourself? What would it mean to lay down the tools for a full Sabbath and discover whether you still feel like someone God loves?

*Source: Nehemiah 4:6; after Bridges, "Who Am I?" — Sabbath as the practice that reveals whether identity is grounded in work or in God.*

**Question 3:** Your introverted wiring means you do your best thinking alone, and your high-Intellect wiring means your thinking is genuinely rigorous and worth trusting. But has it ever made you slow to receive correction? Think of the last time someone challenged a plan or a structure you had built. What happened inside you? The Mason's shadow is not usually anger — it is a quiet, systematic dismissal: "They don't fully understand what I'm trying to do here." Ask someone you respect whether they have ever felt that their pushback on your work was genuinely heard.

*Source: after Powlison, "Seeing with New Eyes" — perception questions that ask how we interpret the voices of others; Proverbs 12:15.*

**Question 4:** You are a builder. But are you building for the body, or are you building a monument? There is a version of the Mason's gift that serves the whole — Bezalel in Exodus 31, whose craftsmanship filled the tabernacle so God might dwell among His people. And there is a version that produces excellent work that no one can touch, critique, adapt, or inherit, because it has quietly become an extension of the self. Name one thing you have built that you have allowed others to use, change, or carry forward without your involvement. If you cannot name one, that is important information.

*Source: Exodus 31:1-6; after Powlison, "Making All Things New" — the question of whose glory the work serves.*

**Question 5:** If you subtracted your ability to complete what others leave unfinished — your Industriousness, your follow-through, your structural patience — would you still know you were worth loving? Say it plainly to yourself: "I am loved by God not because I finish things, but because Christ finished something on my behalf." Read John 19:30: "It is finished." Your best work is always done in response to that word, not in competition with it. This week, tell one person about something you are working on — not to report progress, but simply to let them in.

*Source: John 19:30; after Bridges, "Who Am I?" — the identity question that must be answered from the finished work of Christ, not from ongoing performance.*

## 3. The Reformer

*Wiring: Low Grace-Courtesy + high Assertiveness + high Intellect. Core shadow: directness from the self's edge rather than from grieving love.*

**Question 1:** Think of the last time you named something that needed to be named — a wrong, a compromise, an inconsistency. What was the feeling that preceded the words? Josiah in 2 Kings 22 tears his robes when he hears the law because grief comes first. Ezra in Ezra 9 sits appalled and fasting before he speaks. The prophetic voice that comes from love grieves before it corrects. What comes first in you — the heat of having seen what is wrong, or the weight of mourning that it is so? The answer tells you a great deal about whose agenda is being served.

*Source: 2 Kings 22:11; Ezra 9:3-5; after R.D. Jones, "Uprooting Anger" — the question of whether the emotion beneath the correction is righteous grief or self-referential anger.*

**Question 2:** Ask someone who knows you well — someone who has been on the receiving end of your directness — whether they experience your corrections as coming from someone who is for them or from someone who simply cannot tolerate what is wrong. You may not like what they say. That discomfort is not a sign they are wrong; it may be the most important data point you receive this year. The Reformer who cannot receive feedback about how they deliver feedback has confused the message with the messenger.

*Source: after Powlison, "Seeing with New Eyes" — the question of how our interpretations appear to others; Proverbs 15:31-32.*

**Question 3:** Your low-Courtesy wiring means social friction costs you little. You can say hard things without the relational anxiety that stops most people. That is a genuine gift — the body needs people who are not silenced by the social cost of truth-telling. But notice: do you ever feel something like contempt for those who hesitate to speak? If so, you have moved from gift to shadow. The courage to speak is not superior to the wisdom to wait. Read James 1:19-20: "Let every person be quick to hear, slow to speak, slow to anger; for the anger of man does not produce the righteousness of God." This week, practice being the last one to speak in one conversation.

*Source: James 1:19-20; after R.D. Jones, "Uprooting Anger" — the distinction between righteous passion and the pride that mistakes edge for virtue.*

**Question 4:** Bridges asks in "Who Am I?" whether our identity is in Christ or in our function. For the Reformer, the function is to see what is wrong and say so. But who are you when you are not correcting anyone? When no one needs to be confronted, when the system is working reasonably well, when the moment does not call for your particular wiring — can you rest? Or does a vague restlessness tell you that your sense of purpose is too tightly bound to the role of prophet? Elijah, after the great confrontation at Carmel, fled and wanted to die. God's answer was not a new mission — it was food, sleep, and a gentle question: "What are you doing here?" (1 Kings 19:13). Sit with that question.

*Source: 1 Kings 19:13; after Bridges, "Who Am I?" — the identity question that probes what we believe about ourselves when our gift is not in demand.*

**Question 5:** In your most recent conflict — one in which you said something true and necessary — ask yourself these three questions in sequence: Was what I said true? Was it necessary at that moment? Was it kind? The Reformer often gets the first right and struggles with the second and third. The apostle Paul in Ephesians 4:15 calls the community to speak "the truth in love" — not truth instead of love, and not love that softens truth into nothing, but both at once, held together in the same breath. Name one person to whom you spoke truth recently without enough love. Consider what it would cost you to return and repair.

*Source: Ephesians 4:15; after Powlison, "Making All Things New" — the gospel answer to the Reformer's shadow is not less truth but more love, received first from God.*

## 4. The Herald

*Wiring: Very high Animation (both Enthusiasm and Assertiveness) + high Artistry. Core shadow: proclamation that has become self-referential.*

**Question 1:** After you give a talk, lead worship, or tell a story that lands — when the room responds, when the energy is high, when people are moved — what do you feel first? Gratitude that God used you, or satisfaction that you were good? Both can be present, and the honest answer is often both. But if you sit quietly after the lights go down, which feeling lingers longest? Isaiah 52:7 says, "How beautiful are the feet of him who brings good news" — the beauty is in the feet, the bearing of the message, not in the messenger's arrival. Is there a part of you that has begun to confuse the two?

*Source: Isaiah 52:7; after Welch, "When People Are Big and God Is Small" — the fear-of-man question in reverse: the need for the crowd's response as a form of approval.*

**Question 2:** When was the last time you proclaimed something costly — something that the room did not want to hear, something that risked the warmth you depend on? The Herald's gift is contagious and warm, and that warmth is real. But warmth becomes its own trap when it begins to govern what gets said. John the Baptist in Matthew 3 did not modulate his message for the Pharisees who came to see the show. Nathan in 2 Samuel 12 told David a story that ended in judgment. Both of them risked the relationship. What have you not said because you were unwilling to risk the room's affection?

*Source: Matthew 3:7-8; 2 Samuel 12:7; after Powlison, "Seeing with New Eyes" — the question of which desire is governing the interpretation of the situation.*

**Question 3:** Your high-Animation wiring means you fill rooms. People feel the presence of your energy, your enthusiasm, your generosity of spirit. But does anyone know when you are depleted? There is a version of the Herald that is always "on" — not because they are performing, but because the energy is genuinely there. And there is a version that has confused their public gift with their private self, and no longer knows how to be known as ordinary. Name one person with whom you are reliably un-animated — where you do not have to bring anything — and tell them one true thing about how you are actually doing.

*Source: after Powlison, "Making All Things New" — the X-ray question asks what we believe we need; for the Herald, the need is often for connection that is not contingent on their performance.*

**Question 4:** Read Luke 10:20, the word Jesus gave his disciples after they returned from a mission where even demons obeyed them: "Do not rejoice in this, that the spirits are subject to you, but rejoice that your names are written in heaven." The disciples had just had the kind of success the Herald lives for — impact, results, the evidence that the message worked. And Jesus redirected them from the fruit of their gift to the ground of their security. What would it mean for you to rejoice in that same ground, on a day when nothing you proclaimed seemed to land?

*Source: Luke 10:20; after Bridges, "Who Am I?" — the identity-in-Christ question pressed to the Herald's most vulnerable point.*

**Question 5:** The shadow of very high Animation is that the gift can become the story. You are gifted at giving others language, vision, and courage — but are you directing that language toward Christ, or has it gradually begun to circle back toward yourself? Ask someone who hears you often: "When you walk away from what I've shared, what or who do you most want more of?" If the honest answer is "you," that is not a compliment to receive — it is a diagnosis to take seriously. The goal of proclamation is always John 3:30: "He must increase, but I must decrease."

*Source: John 3:30; after Powlison, "Making All Things New" — the shadow question of whether the gift serves the gospel or the gifted.*

## 5. The Faithful

*Wiring: High Industriousness + steady medium-everything-else. Core shadow: hiddenness as a subtle claim on specialness.*

**Question 1:** You show up. You do the work. You are the one who can be counted on. But ask yourself this: when you show up and no one notices, what happens inside you? Not the surface answer — the real one. The Faithful who is grounded in grace can work unseen without resentment, because the witness of their labor is not the crowd but God. The Faithful who has not yet found that ground will feel a quiet accumulation of grievance — a belief that their faithfulness is going unrecognized and that it ought to be. Read Matthew 6:3-4, where Jesus says the left hand need not know what the right hand gives. Is your hiddenness genuinely free, or is it a form of keeping score?

*Source: Matthew 6:3-4; after Powlison, "Making All Things New" — the X-ray question beneath dutiful behavior.*

**Question 2:** Hebrews 11 is the great honor roll of the Faithful — people who "died in faith, not having received the things promised, but having seen them and greeted them from afar" (Heb. 11:13). They were not recognized in their lifetimes. They did not see the outcomes of their obedience. Their faithfulness was not vindicated by results during their years. What does it do to you to sit with that? Is your steady, long-haul work genuinely untethered from outcome, or does it depend — quietly, carefully — on the eventual arrival of fruit that justifies what you gave?

*Source: Hebrews 11:13; after Bridges, "Who Am I?" — the identity question pressed to the long-haul worker who cannot yet see what God is doing with their faithfulness.*

**Question 3:** If you subtracted your Industriousness — your capacity to keep working when others quit, your quiet reliability, your willingness to take the assignment no one else will hold — would you still know that you bring something the body of Christ needs? The Faithful's gift is real, but it is possible to mistake the gift for the person, and to believe that the only way to matter is to keep producing. Name one gift you carry that is not a function — something you bring to others not by working, but simply by being who you are in Christ.

*Source: after Bridges, "Who Am I?" — the identity question; also 1 Corinthians 12:22, where the body's "weaker" members are given greater honor.*

**Question 4:** When was the last time you asked for help with something you could technically handle alone? The Faithful often move through life as self-sufficient workhorses, not because they are proud in any obvious way, but because dependence feels like weakness and weakness feels dangerous. Welch would call this a shame-adjacent pattern — the belief that your need disqualifies you. But the one who washed his disciples' feet in John 13 also allowed a woman to pour out costly perfume on his head, and rebuked those who called it wasteful. Receiving is not a moral failure. This week, let someone do something for you that you did not need to ask twice.

*Source: John 13:1-17; Mark 14:3-9; after Welch, "Shame Interrupted" — the shame root beneath the pattern of never needing anything from anyone.*

**Question 5:** In your most recent season of sustained, unnoticed faithfulness — the months of showing up to the thing no one applauds — what kept you going? If the honest answer is something like "stubbornness" or "I said I would" or "someone has to," sit with what that reveals. Those are not bad motivations, but they are human ones. The gospel offers a different engine: "Whatever you do, work heartily, as for the Lord and not for men, knowing that from the Lord you will receive the inheritance as your reward" (Col. 3:23-24). Is that the engine under your faithfulness, or is it something you believe in theory but have not yet found in practice?

*Source: Colossians 3:23-24; after Tripp, "New Morning Mercies" — the pastoral question that probes the real motivating center beneath consistent behavior.*

## 6. The Maker

*Wiring: Very high Artistry + medium-high Mastery. Core shadow: making as idolatry rather than offering.*

**Question 1:** Think of the last thing you made that genuinely pleased you — the piece, the design, the arrangement, the written passage, whatever form your making takes. When it was complete, where did you want to take it? Every act of making moves in some direction: toward the Lord as an offering, toward others as a gift, or inward as a private possession. Bezalel in Exodus 31 was filled with the Spirit of God specifically to make beautiful things — the tabernacle that God would inhabit. His making was for a dwelling place for Another. When you finish something, is there a sense of release — a handing over? Or does the made thing become yours in a way that makes it hard to offer?

*Source: Exodus 31:3-5; after Powlison, "Making All Things New" — the X-ray question of where the heart takes what it produces.*

**Question 2:** Read Genesis 1 slowly — the repeated rhythm of "and God saw that it was good." God's making was not anxious, not perfectionist, not self-referential. He made and then he looked at what he made and simply saw that it was good. When you finish something you have made, can you do that? Or does the finished work immediately become an occasion for either self-congratulation or self-criticism — and therefore still about you rather than about the thing itself? The Maker who has found rest in the imago Dei can put down the work and say, simply, "it is good." Is that available to you?

*Source: Genesis 1:10, 12, 18, 21, 25, 31; after Bridges, "Who Am I?" — the question of whether creative identity is rooted in God's prior creative act.*

**Question 3:** When someone does not respond to what you have made — when the piece is overlooked, when the work receives no acknowledgment, when the beauty you offered is simply not seen — what happens in you? The answer reveals what you believe your making is for. If invisibility produces grief that is disproportionate to the occasion, ask whether the making has become a bid for recognition rather than a gift. Welch's question from "When People Are Big and God Is Small" applies here: whose approval is the work actually seeking?

*Source: after Welch, "When People Are Big and God Is Small" — the question of which audience the work is made for; also Colossians 3:23.*

**Question 4:** Your high-Artistry wiring means you perceive the world with unusual sensitivity to form, beauty, and resonance. This is a genuine gift to the body of Christ, which can become dull to beauty and forget that the world is charged with the grandeur of God. But sensitivity to beauty can curdle into aestheticism — a disdain for what is ordinary, plain, or roughly-made. Do you have room in your life for people and places that are not beautiful by your standards? Name one relationship in your life that requires you to set down your aesthetic standards entirely. How does that feel, and what does it tell you?

*Source: after Powlison, "Seeing with New Eyes" — the perception question: what do we see in the world, and what do we overlook?*

**Question 5:** If you could never make another thing — if the gift were taken away by injury, illness, or simply the passage of time — who would you be? This is not a morbid question; it is an identity question. Psalm 139:14 says you are "fearfully and wonderfully made" — the making is God's, and it preceded any making of yours. You were first a made thing before you were a maker. If that ground is solid, the loss of the gift would be grievous but not annihilating. If the ground is not solid, the gift has become something heavier than it was meant to be. What would you need to believe about God in order for that ground to hold?

*Source: Psalm 139:14; after Bridges, "Who Am I?" — the identity-in-Christ question pressed to the person whose gift is bound up in their sense of self.*

## 7. The Attuned

*Wiring: Very high Openness + low Animation (introverted, receptive). Core shadow: interior depth as private superiority.*

**Question 1:** Your very high Openness means you perceive what others miss — undercurrents in conversations, the unspoken grief in a room, the symbolic weight of ordinary things. This is a genuine gift, and the body of Christ needs it. But ask yourself: when you perceive something that others have not seen, what do you do with it? Do you bring it into the light, offering your perception as a gift to the community — or do you hold it privately, accumulating a kind of interior wealth that you rarely spend for anyone else? The gift of perception is not complete until it moves toward another. Read Isaiah 50:4: "He wakens me morning by morning, wakens my ear to listen like one being instructed." The discipled ear is wakened to speak "a word that sustains the weary." What word have you been sitting on?

*Source: Isaiah 50:4; after Powlison, "Seeing with New Eyes" — the perception question that asks whether seeing is finally an act of service.*

**Question 2:** Low Animation means you live quietly, you process internally, and the social cost of entering is real for you. That is not a deficit — it is a particular shape of presence, and there are moments the body of Christ needs exactly this. But: does anyone in your life actually know the depth of what you carry? Or has your interiority become, slowly, a private world that no one enters? Elijah in 1 Kings 19 had gone deeper into the wilderness than he needed to — the cave, the silence, the "I alone am left." God's first move was not to rebuke him but to ask: "What are you doing here?" Not a scolding — a genuine, open question. What would your honest answer be?

*Source: 1 Kings 19:9; after Welch, "Shame Interrupted" — the question of whether interior depth is a protected garden or a place of genuine dwelling-with.*

**Question 3:** Is there anyone in your life who has permission to ask you what you are thinking — not your conclusions, but the actual current of your inner life? The Attuned often processes richly but shares selectively, and the selection can become so narrow that the depth is never shared at all. This is where your gift can become a form of self-protection: the interior world is available only to you, which means it is safe. But "bearing one another's burdens" in Galatians 6:2 requires that you allow your interior weight to be known — not only that you know others'. This week, tell someone what you have actually been thinking about this month.

*Source: Galatians 6:2; after Powlison, "Making All Things New" — the question of what functional belief keeps the Attuned person from moving toward community.*

**Question 4:** The shadow of very high Openness is a kind of private superiority — not arrogance in the common sense, but a quiet sense that one sees more truly than others, perceives more deeply, is less taken in by surface noise. If you are honest, is there anyone in your community whose more conventional, less interior way of being strikes you as shallow? That feeling is important data. It tells you that your gift has shifted from reception to judgment. Read Romans 12:16: "Do not be haughty, but associate with the lowly." Associate — not merely tolerate. Who in your life do you need to learn from, not merely to appreciate from a careful distance?

*Source: Romans 12:16; after Powlison, "Seeing with New Eyes" — the perception question applied to how we perceive other people.*

**Question 5:** The Attuned person is uniquely suited to silence and waiting — the still small voice in 1 Kings 19:12 is exactly the register they operate in. But silence can become passivity. Waiting can become a permanent posture that never moves toward action. Is there a call, a person, a need, or a next step that you have been perceiving for a long time but have not yet acted on — because the noise of action feels foreign to your wiring? The gift of receptivity is real, but it serves the body only when it finally speaks, moves, or shows up. What have you received that it is time to offer?

*Source: 1 Kings 19:12; after Powlison, "Making All Things New" — the call for the Attuned to bring their interior wealth into the community's actual life.*

## 8. The Initiator

*Wiring: High Mastery + high Assertiveness + high steady Ortho-emotion. Core shadow: moving from inability to sit still rather than from divine commission.*

**Question 1:** Think of the last significant initiative you launched — the project you started, the decision you made, the direction you set. In the days before you moved, did you wait for anything? Not for perfect conditions, but for any sense of commission — a word from Scripture, a confirmed counsel, a clear door opening? The Initiator's wiring is genuinely gifted for momentum, and momentum is a gift the body needs. But Saul in 1 Samuel 13 moved without waiting for Samuel because "the people were scattering" and the moment was pressing. The urgency was real. The movement was premature. What would it mean to build a regular practice of waiting before moving?

*Source: 1 Samuel 13:8-14; after Powlison, "Making All Things New" — the X-ray question beneath forward movement: what does the heart believe it cannot bear to wait for?*

**Question 2:** High Assertiveness combined with high Mastery means you are often right about what needs to be done and capable of making it happen. That combination is potent and necessary. But it can produce a pattern in which your rightness is never actually questioned — because you move before there is time to question it, and the results often vindicate you. Ask someone who works closely with you: "Have you ever known that something I was doing was wrong or incomplete, but felt like there was no place to say so?" The answer may be the most important leadership feedback you ever receive.

*Source: after Powlison, "Seeing with New Eyes" — the question of what picture others have of our pattern that we cannot see from inside it; Proverbs 11:14.*

**Question 3:** Joshua 24:15 is the great Initiator's declaration: "As for me and my house, we will serve the Lord." But Joshua had spent forty years in Moses' shadow before that sentence was his to say. He was assistant, student, second-in-command — and the text does not indicate that this diminishment shamed him. Can you name a season when your role was genuinely secondary and you held it well? Or has the Initiator's wiring always found a way to become primary? Being formed for leadership is not the same as being unable to follow. Who are you learning from right now, not as a peer, but as a student?

*Source: Joshua 24:15; Numbers 11:28; after Bridges, "Who Am I?" — the identity question for the person whose gift is direction.*

**Question 4:** In your most recent significant decision — one that affected other people — how many of those people were genuinely consulted before the direction was set? Not informed after, but consulted before. The Initiator who has not grown in this area will often confuse efficiency with wisdom, and "I can see the way forward" with "I have the full picture." Proverbs 15:22 says, "Plans fail for lack of counsel, but with many advisers they succeed." Name one person whose perspective you consistently underconsult. What would it cost you to bring them in earlier next time?

*Source: Proverbs 15:22; after Powlison, "Making All Things New" — the shadow question of whether movement is serving God's purposes or the Initiator's need for movement.*

**Question 5:** Read Hebrews 12:2, the great text of the Initiator's patron: Jesus, "the founder and perfecter of our faith, who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross." The word "founder" in Greek is archēgos — pioneer, trailblazer, one who goes first. This is the pattern behind your wiring. But notice: Jesus' pioneering move was not triumphant in the immediate moment. It was endurance. It was cross-bearing. It was a long, purposeful dying. Does your version of going first include that? When was the last time you pioneered something that cost you, rather than something that rewarded you with results?

*Source: Hebrews 12:2; after Tripp, "New Morning Mercies" — the pastoral question that reorients the Initiator from effectiveness to faithfulness.*

## 9. The Learner

*Wiring: High Openness + high Intellect + externally pursuing. Core shadow: seeking that never arrives, or that defers the cost of knowing.*

**Question 1:** You love to seek. You are drawn to the next idea, the next book, the next framework that might clarify something you have been trying to understand. That is a genuine gift — Proverbs 1:5 calls the wise person one who "hears and increases in learning." But ask yourself honestly: is your seeking oriented toward finding? When you learn something true about God, about yourself, about how to live — do you stop and let it land? Or does the next inquiry begin almost immediately, keeping you in the pleasurable state of pursuit without the demanding weight of arrival? Jesus in Matthew 7:7 says "seek, and you will find." The promise is that seeking has a destination.

*Source: Matthew 7:7; Proverbs 1:5; after Powlison, "Making All Things New" — the X-ray question beneath the pattern of perpetual seeking.*

**Question 2:** Is there something you already know — about God, about yourself, about someone you love — that you have been learning rather than obeying? The Learner's shadow is not ignorance but evasion dressed as inquiry. The next book can defer the application of the last one. The next question can push back the cost of the answer you already received. Read James 1:22-25: "Be doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving yourselves." Name one thing you have known for longer than six months that you have not yet done. Not because you haven't found the right framework — but because you have been afraid to act on it.

*Source: James 1:22-25; after Powlison, "Seeing with New Eyes" — the question of interpretation: what are we telling ourselves about our seeking?*

**Question 3:** Very high Openness means you are drawn to the new, the unexpected, the unconventional. That makes you a gifted explorer and a genuinely interesting companion. But it can produce a restlessness with the ordinary that makes sustained commitment difficult. Are there any practices, communities, or callings in your life that have not changed significantly in the past five years — that you have remained in not because they are stimulating but because they are yours? The Learner who can only stay where the learning is new has not yet discovered the learning that comes only from rootedness.

*Source: after Bridges, "Who Am I?" — the identity question pressed to the person who finds their energy in movement; also Psalm 131.*

**Question 4:** Who do you learn from, and what are they like? The Learner tends to gravitate toward teachers, writers, and thinkers — toward ideas more than toward people whose primary gift is not intellectual. Ask yourself: is there anyone in your life whose primary form of wisdom is not cognitive — a person of patient faithfulness, a person of simple holiness, a person who would not be interesting at a dinner party but who carries something you need? Name one such person and tell them something you have learned from watching them.

*Source: after Powlison, "Seeing with New Eyes" — the question of what we fail to perceive because we are looking for something else; 1 Corinthians 1:27.*

**Question 5:** Colossians 2:3 says that in Christ "are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge." The Learner who has found this verse has found the deepest orientation for their wiring: the whole treasury of what they most want is located in a Person. Not in a library, not in a framework, not in the next conversation. Has your learning life drawn you toward that Person — toward more of Christ himself — or has it gradually become an ecosystem that runs on its own momentum, circling richer and richer ideas without arriving at Him? What would it mean to measure the health of your learning life by how much closer to Christ it is taking you?

*Source: Colossians 2:3; after Bridges, "Who Am I?" — the identity question that asks whether the Learner's hunger is oriented toward its proper object.*

## 10. The Servant

*Wiring: Medium-low across most domains + high Grace-bearing relative. Core shadow: invisibility as a fixed identity rather than a free gift.*

**Question 1:** You are wired for the behind-the-scenes work that makes everything else possible. You notice what needs doing and you do it, often without being asked. That is a genuine gift, and the body of Christ would be impoverished without it. But ask yourself this: when someone tries to honor you publicly, to name what you have contributed, to make your service visible — what happens inside you? Discomfort is understandable. But if what you feel is something more like anger, or a quick and practiced deflection, ask whether your hiddenness has become an identity you are protecting. Read John 13:12-15, where Jesus names and dignifies what he has just done — he does not hide the washing, he teaches from it. True servant-hood can be seen.

*Source: John 13:12-15; after Welch, "Shame Interrupted" — the question of whether invisibility is a gift or a self-protective reflex.*

**Question 2:** If you subtracted your service — your willingness to take the task no one else takes, your readiness to be in the background, your practical faithfulness to the needs of the community — would you still know that you have something to offer? Welch's work in "Shame Interrupted" names the experience of people who believe their worth is entirely contingent on their usefulness. The gospel says something different: you are loved before you serve, not because you serve. Read Philippians 2:7, which describes Christ taking the form of a servant — but note that he did so "though he was in the form of God." His service did not define him; it expressed him. Is yours expressing you, or defining you?

*Source: Philippians 2:6-7; after Welch, "Shame Interrupted" — the core shame question: is my worth contingent on my usefulness?*

**Question 3:** When was the last time you brought a need of your own into a community setting — not a need you could handle quietly, but one that required someone else to rearrange their plans for you? The Servant who cannot receive is not fully free. There is something that feels like virtue in the pattern of never needing anything, but it may be protecting a deeper belief that your need would be unwelcome, or that the cost of being cared for is that you owe something you cannot repay. Read Galatians 6:2 in its full reciprocity: "Bear one another's burdens." The one is as commanded as the other. Name one burden you are carrying that you have not yet allowed anyone else to help bear.

*Source: Galatians 6:2; after Welch, "When People Are Big and God Is Small" — the question of what it would mean to let others see your need.*

**Question 4:** Your medium-low Animation means you do not naturally fill rooms or lead conversations, and your medium-low Assertiveness means you are not usually the one to set the agenda. Those are not deficits — they are the wiring of the one who makes space for everyone else. But: is there anything God has given you to say that you have not said because you do not believe you are the kind of person who says things like that? The Servant's shadow is not only self-effacement in service; it is a failure to offer what is genuinely yours to offer. Bridges' question applies: "Who are you in Christ?" — not "Who are you in the room?" Name one thing you carry that the community needs from you, not from anyone else.

*Source: after Bridges, "Who Am I?" — the identity question pressed toward agency, not self-assertion; 1 Corinthians 12:22.*

**Question 5:** Read Matthew 25:21, the commendation the Faithful Servant receives: "Well done, good and faithful servant. You have been faithful over a little; I will set you over much." The Servant in the parable was not praised for minimizing themselves. They were praised for faithfulness — for taking what they were given and using it fully. Is there any area of your life where your posture of hiddenness has become an excuse not to bring everything you have? The question is not whether you should serve — you should, and you are gifted for it — but whether you are serving fully, from all of what God has entrusted to you, or whether you have been hiding part of it for reasons that feel like humility but may be something else.

*Source: Matthew 25:21; after Powlison, "Making All Things New" — the X-ray question beneath the servant's self-diminishment; also after Welch, "Shame Interrupted," on the hidden belief that one's full presence would be unwelcome.*
